Before we start

Know your needs.

Before diving into specific features, assess your playing style and skill level. Are you a beginner getting started, an intermediate player seeking consistency, or an advanced competitor wanting a racket that amplifies your strengths? That answer will save you ₹20,000 and a lot of frustration.

CHAPTER 01
Player skill level

Be honest about your level.

The single biggest mistake new padel players make is buying a pro racket because the pro plays with it. A racket that's too advanced punishes inconsistent technique — your shots get harder to control, not easier. Match the racket to the swing you have today, not the one you wish you had.

Beginner Light · Forgiving

Pick lightweight rackets that are easy to handle, with larger sweet spots for better control and forgiveness on off-center hits.

Intermediate Balanced

Look for a balance between power and control. You're refining technique — you want a racket that rewards good form without punishing bad days.

Advanced Precision · Power

Advanced players prefer rackets that deliver precision and control, leveraging refined techniques and timing to extract maximum performance.

CHAPTER 02
Racket shape

Shape changes everything.

Padel rackets come in three shapes — and the shape determines where the sweet spot sits, how much power you get for free, and how forgiving the frame is when you mis-hit. Pick the wrong shape and no amount of grip or core will save you.

Round Sweet spot · Control

Larger sweet spot, better control. Ideal for beginners and defensive players. Forgiving on off-center hits while you build technique.

Teardrop Hybrid · Versatile

The all-rounder. Balanced between power and control. Suited to intermediate players who switch between attack and defense in the same point.

Diamond Power · Pro

Sweet spot moves up the head. More power and speed, but requires precise contact. Favored by advanced finishers and bandeja specialists.

CHAPTER 03
Weight

Lighter to learn,
heavier to finish.

Most adult padel rackets sit between 350g and 390g. The difference between 360g and 380g feels small on the shelf — but in hour two of a tournament, it's the difference between fresh and fried. Default lighter when in doubt.

Lightweight < 360g

Easy maneuverability. Quick reactions at the net. Reduces arm fatigue across long sessions. Best for beginners and control-first players.

Medium 360 – 375g

The sweet spot for most intermediate players. Enough mass for power without sacrificing the ability to whip the racket through volleys.

Heavy 375g +

Maximum power and stability on contact. Demands real arm strength and clean technique. Reserved for advanced players who finish points.

CHAPTER 04
Balance

Where the weight sits.

Two rackets can weigh the same and feel completely different. The balance — where the weight is distributed along the frame — determines whether the racket feels nimble in your hand or wants to swing through the ball on its own. Pick balance, not just weight.

Low (Head-Light) Maneuver

Weight in the handle. Easier to swing, better wrist control. Suited to beginners and defensive players who play more volleys than smashes.

Even All-round

Weight distributed equally. The most adaptable feel — works across attack and defense. Default choice for intermediates.

High (Head-Heavy) Power

Weight up top. The racket loads on the swing and releases more energy into the ball. Suited to players with faster swings and aggressive baseline games.

CHAPTER 05
Core material

The engine inside.

The core is the foam sandwiched between the two faces. It's where the racket's character lives — firm and snappy, or soft and absorbent. Two players can pick the same shape and weight; the core is often what makes one walk away happy and the other return it.

EVA Foam Firm · Powerful

Denser, firmer feel. More power on contact, faster ball-off-face response. Suited to players who want a solid, planted hit.

Polyethylene Foam Soft · Comfort

Softer feel, more cushioning, less vibration. Easier on the elbow and shoulder. Ideal for touch players and anyone with joint sensitivity.

CHAPTER 06
Surface material

What touches the ball.

The face material decides whether the ball pops off the racket or sticks to it. Fiberglass faces are forgiving; carbon faces reward technique. Most pro rackets are carbon — but that doesn't mean most players should buy them.

Fiberglass Soft · Control

Softer feel. Better control and a more comfortable hit. Suited to beginners and intermediate players prioritizing precision over raw power.

Carbon Fiber Stiff · Power

Stiffer face — the ball jumps. More power, more durability, sharper feedback. Ideal for advanced players with consistent contact.

CHAPTER 07
Grip size

Comfort starts in the hand.

The wrong grip size is the #1 cause of tennis elbow in padel players. Too small and you'll over-grip and tense the forearm; too large and you'll lose wrist snap and contact precision. Most adult players in India fit a medium — but measure if you can.

Small 4 – 4⅛"

Smaller hands. Allows for natural wrist action and finer control on touch shots. Pair with one overgrip if you want a touch more width.

Medium 4¼ – 4⅜"

The most common adult size. Balanced grip — secure without locking the wrist. If you're unsure, this is your default.

Large 4½" +

Larger hands. Stability through aggressive swings. Reduces wrist strain on heavy smashes.

CHAPTER 08
Surface texture

Spin or precision.

Run your finger over the racket face. Smooth means the ball slides cleanly — predictable, accurate, easier to place. Textured means the ball grabs — more spin potential, more variety on returns, more chaos for your opponent.

Smooth Predictable

Better control and shot placement. Suited to players who win with precision and angles, not topspin.

Textured Spin

Enhanced grip on the ball. Generates more spin on serves and returns. Ideal for players who rely on spin to dictate rallies.
